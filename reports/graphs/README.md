# Report graphs

Generated by `make plots` (`scripts/plot_report.py`) from `reports/benchmark.csv`, `gmu_published.csv`,
`profile.csv` and the sweep. All numbers: xc7a100tftg256-2L, Vivado 2026.1, out of context, LWC wrapper included
(the setup of GMU's benchmark, ePrint 2023/484).

The figures make the argument in order: where every core lands (01, 01b), how we got there (02), why it stops
there (03), why unrolling alone wasn't the answer (05), and how the best cores rank against all the LWC
finalists (07, 07b).

**Reading them.**
- **Color and marker = who built the core:** Brody orange diamonds, Logan blue squares, merged green circles,
  GMU's finalists gray, Elephant_GMU and the base cores near-black. The colors stay distinguishable under the three
  common color-vision deficiencies, and labels repeat the owner anyway.
- **Final designs:** `brody/r4_BtoJ`, `brody/r2_BtoL`, `merged/ring40_IL` (fastest), `merged/ring32_ILN`
  (best Kbps/LUT), `merged/ring20_IL` (smallest ring). Ring names count the rounds in the ring.
- **Why `base_r1` appears:** it is `base_r10` at 1 round per cycle, the setting of the published Elephant_GMU,
  and matches it on cycles per block (170 vs about 171) and 16 B throughput (80 Mbps each). So `base_r10`'s ×2.6
  over Elephant_GMU comes from GMU's unroll default, not from our work.

## 01_landscape.png: throughput vs area
Long-message throughput vs LUTs for every core, against Elephant_GMU. Dashed rays mark constant Kbps/LUT; the
staircase is the Pareto front.
- The front runs Elephant_GMU → Logan r4 → r4_BtoC → ring16 → ring20 → ring20_IL → ring32_ILN → ring40_IL.

## 01b_long_vs_short.png: long vs 16-byte messages
Long-message throughput (x) vs 16 B throughput (y), one dot per core.
- Front: r4_B → Logan r4 → r4_BtoI → ring40_IL. The small iterative cores win at 16 B (271 / 265 Mbps); the
  rings win on long messages (8021 Mbps) but drop to about 220 Mbps at 16 B; r4_BtoI/J is the compromise
  (5909 / 254). Our r2 cores give up the most at 16 B (146–175 Mbps).

## 02_progress.png: every step of both lineages
Long-message throughput (top) and Kbps/LUT (bottom): base_r1, base_r10, the r4 path B–J, r2_BtoL, then each of
Logan's cores followed by what we built on it. Header: speedup over base_r10.
- **Throughput:** B–G take base_r10 from 539 to 5219 Mbps (×9.7); H–J and the r2 line reach ×12.5. Our I and L
  take Logan's ring40 from ×13 to ×15 (7162 → 8021 Mbps), ring20 from ×7.9 to ×8.9.
- **Efficiency moves later:** flat at about 460 Kbps/LUT through C–F, then G and H take it to 1029; option N
  (ring32_ILN) reaches 1546.

## 03_bottleneck.png: why it stops at 5 cycles per block
Left: PT (bar) and AD (dot) cycles per 20 B block, with the 5-cycle bus limit. Right: share of cycles where the
permutation runs while a word moves, permutation only, word only, neither.
- base_r10 leaves the permutation idle 44% of the time. From G on, a word moves in over 90% of cycles: the core
  is I/O-bound, and only Fmax can raise throughput. That's why the higher-clock rings win.
- Logan's cores show the same: ring16 and ring20 stop at 10.0 and 8.1 cycles per block; only ring40 reaches the
  bus limit.

## 05_unroll.png: unrolling alone
Fmax, LUTs and throughput vs rounds per cycle for the `base_rN` cores, the sweep (Fmax panel) and Elephant_GMU.
- Unrolling tops out at ×1.11 over base_r10 (R = 8): above R ≈ 4 the rounds set the clock, so Fmax falls as fast
  as the cycle count drops. The speedup had to come from architecture (02).

## 07_rankings.png: against GMU's finalists (plaintext)
Three ranked panels (16 B throughput, long throughput, long Kbps/LUT) over GMU's 30 unprotected designs plus
the merged cores, base_r10 and base_r1.
- **Long messages:** ring40_IL and ring32_ILN (8021 / 8000 Mbps) beat every finalist (best: Ascon-128a GMU2-x2,
  5421). ring20_IL (4807) is beaten only by that Ascon design.
- **Kbps/LUT:** ring32_ILN (1546) ranks below 7 designs (Xoodyak GMU2-x1 leads at 3154).
- **16 B:** the merged cores (217–222 Mbps) rank below 24 designs.

## 07b_rankings_ad.png: against GMU's finalists (associated data)
Long AD throughput and AD Kbps/LUT. GMU's AD throughput is derived as Table 14's AD Kbps/LUT × LUTs.
- The merged cores (7744–7951 Mbps) are second only to Xoodyak GMU2-x1 (8513).
- Per LUT, ring20_IL (2004) ranks below 4 designs: it reaches the bus limit on AD (5.04 cycles per block) with
  half the rounds of ring40, since AD needs one permutation per block and PT two.

## Sources and regenerating
- GMU values: Mohajerani et al., ePrint 2023/484 (`docs/SCA-Benchmarking.pdf`), Tables 12 and 14, unprotected
  implementations. `reports/gmu_published.csv` is parsed from the PDF text, not retyped. Elephant_GMU is spec v1.
- matplotlib isn't in the system Python (and `~/.local` lacks `packaging`). Install it elsewhere and skip the user
  site-packages: `python3 -m pip install --target /tmp/pylib matplotlib`, then
  `PYTHONPATH=/tmp/pylib python3 -s scripts/plot_report.py`.
