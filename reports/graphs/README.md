# Report graphs

Generated by `make plots` (`scripts/plot_report.py`) from `reports/benchmark.csv`, `gmu_published.csv`,
`profile.csv` and the design-space sweep. All numbers are on xc7a100tftg256-2L (Vivado 2026.1, out of context, LWC
wrapper included), the part GMU used.

The figures make the report's argument in order: where every core lands (01, 01b), how we got there step by step
(02), why it stops where it does (03), why unrolling alone was not the answer (05), and how the best designs rank
against GMU's benchmark of all the LWC finalists (07, 07b).

**Color = who built the core**, in every figure: Brody's cores orange diamonds, Logan's cores blue squares,
merged cores light green circles (with a darker green outline, since the light green has low contrast on the white
background). GMU's finalist designs are gray; Elephant_GMU and the bases are ink. The three owner colors stay at least
ΔE 10 apart (OKLab ×100) under deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia; marker shapes and direct labels repeat the
owner anyway.

**base_r1 is in the figures** as the like-for-like reference for Elephant_GMU. `brody/base_r1` and `brody/base_r10`
are the same v2 port of GMU's arch-1 core at 1 and 10 rounds per cycle: base_r1 has the setting of the published
Elephant_GMU, base_r10 keeps the default of GMU's source and is the baseline of every speedup. base_r1 matches
Elephant_GMU's cycles per block (170 vs about 171) and 16 B throughput (80 vs 80 Mbps); it is slower on long messages
only through its clock (168 vs 222 MHz). So base_r10's ×2.6 over Elephant_GMU comes from the inherited unroll factor,
not from our work. Elephant_GMU itself was the 1-round unprotected starting design GMU and Bochum masked in ePrint
2023/484 (§7.1, Table 5). In 03 base_r1's 170-cycle bar is cut at the axis edge, with its values printed on it.

**Final designs**: `brody/r4_BtoJ`, `brody/r2_BtoL`, `merged/ring40_IL` (fastest), `merged/ring32_ILN` (best Kbps/LUT;
32 rounds in a 16×2 ring), `merged/ring20_IL` (smallest ring). Ring names count the rounds in the ring
(`rtl/README.md`).

## Sources
- GMU values: Mohajerani et al., *SCA Evaluation and Benchmarking of Finalists in the NIST Lightweight
  Cryptography Standardization Process*, ePrint 2023/484 (`docs/SCA-Benchmarking.pdf`), Tables 12 and 14,
  unprotected implementations. `reports/gmu_published.csv` is parsed from the PDF text, not retyped.
  Elephant_GMU is GMU's own Elephant (spec v1; see `rtl/brody/base_r1/README.md` for how it compares with ours).
- Cycle-accurate numbers come from Questa runs of the LWC testbench (`make cycles`, `make profile`).

## Regenerating
- matplotlib is not in the system Python (and the copy in `~/.local` is missing `packaging`). Install it somewhere
  and point `PYTHONPATH` at it, e.g. `python3 -m pip install --target /tmp/pylib matplotlib`, then
  `PYTHONPATH=/tmp/pylib python3 -s scripts/plot_report.py` (`-s` skips the broken user site-packages).
